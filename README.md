# Parental schooling and children's outcomes

This repository contains the analysis code and selected results from my thesis on the intergenerational effects of Turkey's 1997 compulsory schooling reform. I use the January 1987 parental birth-date cutoff. The first stage examines whether reform exposure increased parents' completion of eight years of schooling.

Before studying children's outcomes, I examine sample selection across age intervals. If reform exposure changes the probability that a parent has a child in a given age range, an estimated effect for children in that range may be misleading even when it is statistically significant. The sample-selection tables separate selection-biased intervals from those without detectable selection. This step guides which ages are most informative for the mechanisms.

I then explore two possible mechanisms. The first is children's development, beginning with early-childhood measures based on [UNICEF's Early Childhood Development Index 2030](https://data.unicef.org/resources/early-childhood-development-index-2030-ecdi2030/), nutrition, and the [WHO Child Growth Standards](https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards). Alongside the age 2-4 and 0-6 analyses, I report the early-childhood intervals with no detectable sample-selection discontinuity: ages 0-2, 0-3, and 1-3. I also use the [WHO 2007 growth reference](https://www.who.int/tools/growth-reference-data-for-5to19-years) for primary-school children. The second mechanism is school success in primary school, alongside possible inputs such as study time, household work, and education spending. The repository also retains middle- and high-school analyses as extensions. Taken together, the results do not identify a statistically convincing channel that validates either proposed mechanism.

The main outcome tables use the youngest eligible child and age at the end of the survey, 16 December 2022; sample selection uses the any-child rule. Alternative reference dates and oldest-child specifications are under `additional checks`. The confidential microdata and intermediate datasets are not included.
